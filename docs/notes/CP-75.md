## Decisions

- **What "the lessons still to place" are.** Each limit's *fewest* (a week or a fortnight), never less than what is already there. A
  subject with no "fewest" gets nothing new. **On the live database today no class has any limit row**, so Fill has nothing to place
  until a coordinator enters the weekly counts in the rules panel ("Maths: 6 a week").
- **What is kept.** A cell a person set, every fixed cell, and every cell of a class that is not being filled. A machine-made cell may
  move, and each one that does costs a change. The Build page fills the class on screen; the other classes are read so that an educator
  is never in two places.
- **What it optimises**, in order: fewest broken Try to rules (by the rule's weight), fewest cells changed, then a subject spread evenly
  over its days (a tie-break only; it is not a rule and never beats one).
- **Specialists.** `timetable.specialists` (seeded empty by #866, and empty on live) is read as a list of head codes. While it is empty
  every new lesson is placed as the class's own educator's, and the result says so in `notes`.
- **Cannot be met.** Arithmetic first (a kept lesson already breaks a Must rule; a subject needs more than its free places; a class needs
  more than it has), with no solve. Otherwise one more solve where every editable Must (an "at most", an absence, a "fewest") may bend
  at a high cost, and the rules that gave are said in words. Built-in rules (one class at a time, one educator at a time, a person's
  cell) never bend.
- **An answer the checker rejects** is thrown away; the caller sees outcome `failed`. `timetable_run.outcome` allows only CP-11's three
  words and this issue has no schema change, so the row is kept as `not_found_in_time` with `report.failure` and `report.faults` saying
  why. A fourth outcome word would be a one-line check change in a later migration.
- **"At most N of one subject in a day" for every subject** (subject `*`, the checker's `ANY`) is one cap per subject the class has.
- **Arithmetic refusals say each thing once**: when one subject alone does not fit, the class's week and fortnight totals are not listed too.
- **A Must problem that already stood wholly inside classes not being filled** is not the answer's fault and does not fail it.
- **Using an answer** is refused (409) when the pattern or the rules changed since it was worked out; the same answer is not used twice.
- **FET stays the generator's default.** The parity is proven by `solver="cpsat"`; the switch (and taking FET out of the image) is one
  line when asked for. Two of CP-11's claims cannot carry over and are said in the test: "the kept files are FET's own, replayed through
  fet-cl", and the input FET searched until its time limit, which CP-SAT proves impossible at once (so out-of-time is shown with a solver
  that does run out).

## Found on the way

- **The old CP-11 and CP-6 proof worlds were broken on main**: CP-71 changed the period key to include `valid_from`, and
  `specs/CP-11/proofs/cp11_world.py` and `specs/CP-6/proofs/cp6_world.py` still named the old key in `on conflict`, so every proof that
  builds that world failed before reaching its subject. One line each, fixed here.
- Adding `ortools` moved `protobuf` from 7.36 to 6.33 in `uv.lock`. It cannot be avoided: OR-Tools 9.15 (the newest) requires
  `protobuf>=6.33.1,<6.34`, and the only other user, the digit reader's `paddlepaddle`, asks only for `>=3.20.2`. Proven under the final
  lock (protobuf 6.33.6), with the real reader and its real models, on images the tests draw themselves (no child data):
  `tests/test_digits.py` 6 passed, `tests/test_boxes.py` 23 passed (its bent-page tests run the real reader on a printed, "scanned" paper).
  Both files run in the nightly suite.
- `spylls` installs a top-level `tests` package into the virtualenv; a script outside pytest that does `from tests import ...` gets that
  one. Pytest is not affected.
