## Group 3, step 3-prep: make the moves safe before making them

Plain English: nothing in the database changes in this step. It gets four things ready for the three steps that move
approvals, proposals and evals (3A, 3B, 3C), so each of those can be checked properly and changed in one place.

- **The "nothing changed for past assessments" check looks at what a question and a level say, not the whole row.**
  `engine past --curriculum` fingerprinted every column of `item` and `skill_level`, so adding a column, touching
  `updated_at` or mirroring an approval would have looked like a change to the curriculum. It now lists the columns that
  carry meaning (`checks/past.py`, `CURRICULUM`). Left out: `updated_at` (a clock), `times_used` and `p_correct` (usage
  the graph recounts), and a level's `confirmed_by` / `confirmed_at` (an approval stamp, mirrored into `approval` in 3A
  and dropped in group 4; the `approval_list` and `drawable_item` comparisons guard those). A baseline taken with the
  old version does not compare with this one: take both sides with this code, as the rehearsal does.
- **The curriculum's commands read only curriculum proposals.** `map_ledger.standing()` (so `proposed`, `settle`,
  `apply`, `revert` and `engine curriculum review`) and `stopped()` read only the kinds `_link` makes a link from
  (`map_ledger.MAPPING`), and `_link` refuses any other kind instead of treating it as `levels`. A bank proposal moved
  into `curriculum_proposal` in 3B is never listed or applied through `engine curriculum`.
- **One test helper per place an approval lives.** Engine `tests/rows.py`: `a_waiting_type`, `no_type_approved`,
  `an_approved_type`, `a_retired_question`, `an_approved_paper`; website `tests/rows.ts`: `aWaitingWay`,
  `anApprovedWay`, `forgetFlags`. The tests that deleted or inserted `question_type_approval` / `item_feedback` rows
  directly use them now (`test_question_types`, `test_e0_english`, `test_english_edit`, `test_refill_fair`,
  `test_story_keys`, `test_child_move`; `e0-english`, `u55`, `s3-worksheets`, `e2e`); what each asserts is unchanged.
  `forgets_what_it_commits` also cleans a table whose clock is `at` (`change_log` has no `created_at`).
  Not moved: `test_approval_list.py` (CP-7 item 8 holds it unchanged against main; 3A rewrites the list), and the
  browser specs that insert papers with `approved_by` (the stamp stays through group 3, mirrored by a trigger; their
  move goes with group 4, which drops it).
- **The rehearsal and measure scripts survive a table leaving.** `.github/measure/{b2_types,sf2_kinds,sf2_formats}.py`

## Group 3, step 3A: every yes or no is a row of the one approval list

Plain English: four small tables that each kept one kind of "yes" or "no" are gone from the working schema. Their
rows now live in `approval`, the one list of decisions, each tagged with where it came from. A way of asking still has
to be approved before a paper uses it; a question someone flags is still taken out of the bank at once; a report still
needs two different people before it can be sent; "I've looked at this" still hides a level until its numbers move; a
class card confirmed is now seen only by whoever sees that class. The approvals still written on their own row (a
level's, a report's, a paper's, a link's) stay there until group 4, and each one now also writes a row of the list, so
the list is the one place to read. Nothing a child did, and no question or level, changed: checked on a rehearsal.

Migration `20270125220000_cp27_one_approval_list.sql`, one transaction under `db push`. Tables in `public` and `pii`:
142 → 138; functions in `public`, `internal` and `pii`: 129 → 129 (one added, `internal.approval_from_stamp`; one
dropped, `internal.question_types_start_approved`, the one-time start of B2 whose rows are approval rows now).

| Table | Live rows (census) | Now | Where it went |
|---|---|---|---|
| `question_type_approval` | 74 | retired | `approval` kind `question_type`, decision `approved`, same person and time |
| `item_feedback` | 7,728 | retired | `approval` kind `item` (target the question's id), decision `retire` / `keep`, the note its `reason` (a blank note no reason); the trigger that retires a flagged question, `internal.retire_flagged_item`, sits on `approval` |
| `class_card_confirmation` | 0 | retired | `approval` kind `class_card` (target `section week`), decision `confirmed`, the card as seen in `detail` |
| `calibration_review` | 0 | retired | `approval` kind `calibration` (target `skill level scope_kind scope`), decision `looked_at`, the counts in `detail` |

Every moved row carries `detail ->> 'kept_in'` = the table (or `table.column` for a stamp) it came from, and the
migration counts them. The two empty tables are copied and retired like the others, not dropped: "I've looked at this"
and a class card can be written on live until the moment the migration runs.

**The list.** `approval` gains `detail` (what a decision was taken on) and `seq` (the order decisions were taken in:
rows written in one transaction share a clock). Its kinds and decisions are rules on the columns: kinds `skill_level`,
`question_type`, `parent_note`, `sheet`, `skill_prerequisite`, `item`, `calibration`, `class_card`; decisions
`approved`, `sent_back`, `withdrawn` for the first five, `retire`/`keep` for a question, `looked_at`, `confirmed`.
- `approval_list` (same columns, same answers) reads `approval` alone: a first approval is the first `approved` after
  the latest `withdrawn`; a second is someone else's since then; a send-back counts after the first approval, as before.
- The stamps stay until group 4 (option M of the map): `skill.level_approved` (through `skill_level.confirmed_*`),
  `parent_note.approved_by`, `sheet_instance.approved_*`, `skill_prerequisite.confirmed_*`. One trigger function,
  `internal.approval_from_stamp`, writes `approved` when one is given and `withdrawn` for what stood when one is taken
  back, given again or its row renamed. Every stamp already given was copied with the time the list showed for it (a
  report's: the note's last change, as the list read it).
- `approval_checked` is kind-aware: once something stands approved, another approval is a second one and must be
  someone else's; a first approval of something stamped on its own row must be the one its row names (a way of asking
  is approved in the list itself). `parent_note_sent_when_approved_twice` is unchanged and reads the new list.
- `drawable_item` asks `approval` for "an approved way of asking" (an approval no withdrawal came after). Through
  `approval_list` the planner joined the ways before the questions' kinds were known: 975 ms against 44 ms; 52-58 ms
  this way. The migration runs `analyze approval` (without statistics it took 12 s).
- `level_kind_choice` joins `approval_list`; `level_calibration` reads the latest `calibration` row.
- Access: an approval of a report, a paper or a class card is seen by whoever sees that report (reach on `reports`),
  paper (`papers`) or class (`learning`, through `detail ->> 'section'`); every other kind is the school's, open to
  every educator of it, as the tables it came from were (school-wide). `app_web` may add an approval (the website
  approves a way of asking and records "I've looked at this", as it did into the old tables).
- A question deleted after it was flagged (`legacy paper` deletes a question its file no longer names): its flag stayed
  with it (`item_feedback` cascaded) and now stays as an `approval` row naming an id no question has. What reads flags
  (`refill.reinstate`, the question page) starts from a question, so none shows it (`test_bank`).

**Order of operations.** 1. Refuse (one block, raises naming each): an `approval` kind or decision outside today's,
an unknown verdict, a blank flag actor or class-card name, a level approved with no name or time
(`level_approved` or `skill_level.confirmed_*`), a blank report or paper approver, a link confirmed by no one; then a
notice of the counts (ways, flags and how many `keep`, cards, looks, levels, reports, papers and links approved).
2. Keep, in temporary tables, what reads an approval: `approval_list`, `drawable_item` ids, `level_kind_choice`,
`level_calibration`, and a checksum of every question's status. 3. Widen `approval` (kinds, decisions, `detail`,
`seq`). 4. With the list's triggers off: copy every row (the moved tables and the stamps) in the order they were made,
then number the decisions already there after them; triggers on; `seq` an identity; the index on `seq`; `analyze`.
5. The new `approval_list`, `approval_checked`, `approval_from_stamp` and its eight triggers (insert and update on four
tables), `retire_flagged_item` moved onto `approval` (after the copy, so no flag touched a question again).
6. `drawable_item`, `level_kind_choice`, `level_calibration`; drop `question_types_start_approved`. 7. Count checks,
one per source: the rows joined back on every value (tenant, what, decision, who, reason, card or counts, when, tag)
equal the source's count, and the tagged rows equal it too; each stamp one `approved` row of the same person; the five
outputs of step 2 equal (both ways); any difference raises. 8. Grants (`app_web` insert) and `apply_conventions` with
the new access rule. 9. `access_table` rows out, `data_register` names `approval` for the class card, then (as groups 1
and 2) no key from a table that stays into one that goes, their keys, triggers and policies dropped, `set schema
retired`, grants revoked.

## Group 5: the timetable is the dated lessons; weeks become dates

Plain English: a class's timetable for a day is now simply its lessons for that day, nothing else. The week A/B frame
the timetable is drafted from, the days nothing may go on and the most or fewest lessons of a subject now sit in one
table of rules, so FET is asked exactly what it was asked before. A fortnight's state (planning, frozen, aligned,
approved, list sent) is part of the fortnight; each step someone takes, a class's fortnight approved, a draft put live
and a clash accepted are rows of the one approval list; a lesson moved by hand and a change made after a fortnight froze
are rows of the one history log. Every "week" the school's papers, reports and plans carry is now a date (the Monday of
that week); an old paper's own label ("Sep W1", "Baseline") stays with the paper. Twelve tables leave (two kept aside
with their rows, ten that were empty dropped); one arrives. On a copy shaped like live, every date's lessons, every
child's results, every question and level, FET's input and every week are the same before and after.

Migration `20270125260000_cp27_time.sql`, one transaction under `db push`. Tables in `public` and `pii`: 138 → 127 (12
leave, `timetable_rule` arrives); functions in `public`, `internal` and `pii`: 129 → 124 (10 dropped:
`fortnight_draft`, `fortnight_approve(plan)`, `timetable_fortnight_layer`, `fortnight_slots`, `fortnight_lines`,
`fortnight_signature`, `fortnight_signature_of_slots`, `fortnight_signature_of_plan`, `fortnight_plan_guard`,
`fortnight_plan_no_delete`; 5 made: `timetable_signature`, `timetable_standing`, `fortnight_approve(class, fortnight)`,

**The lessons.** `session` gains `kind` (`fixed`, `subject`, `specialist`): the frame's cell on its date (week A the first

**CP-7 on the lessons.** `timetable_signature(class, fortnight)` is each lesson's place (the week counted from the
fortnight's first day, so a 15-day fortnight's third Monday is its own, and the weekday), period, kind, subject and words
