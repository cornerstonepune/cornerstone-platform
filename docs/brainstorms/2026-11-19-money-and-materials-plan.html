Money & Materials in Teacher OS: the plan

How this becomes part of Teacher OS: who does what, in what rhythm, what the system does on its own, and the order we build it in. It is grounded in what is already in the Teacher OS database today (checked on 19 Nov) and in the expense history from the review. Decisions you need to make are on the last tab.

Three layers, built in this order

LayerWhat it isWhy this order
1. MoneyEvery rupee spent is logged with its receipt, category, campus and approver at the moment it is spent. Cash floats replace staff paying from their own pockets.This is where the ₹5.7 L a year is, and it happens every day. Nothing else needs to exist first.
2. Plans & vendorsBudgets per category, per campus and per event. Recurring vendors (tanker, garbage, tea, pest control, RO, printing) are paid on a schedule. Capex is approved before purchase and recorded as an asset.Once money is captured cleanly, comparing plan with actual becomes automatic.
3. MaterialsThe timetable generates each fortnight's materials list. The store keeps minimum stock levels, educators collect from labelled bins, and stock is counted once a term.This depends on a materials catalogue and on the timetable linking to activities, which only 3 of 1,049 sessions do today. Building it last avoids rework.

Educator flow

Educators touch the money side only rarely. Their main job is the fortnight materials list, and that takes minutes.

A. Every fortnight: materials (≈ 5 min)

WhenEducator doesSystem does
Fortnight timetable lockedNothing.Builds the class's materials list from the planned activities' materials, e.g. “12 chart papers, 2 tapes, 1 kg maida, Mon 24 Nov”. Before linking is live, it asks the educator to tick the activities they will run.
By the Wednesday before the fortnightOpens My materials on their phone. Removes what they don't need, adds anything missing (search the catalogue, or type free text), then taps Submit.Checks every line against store stock. Each line is marked In store (with its bin), To buy or Already in class.
After the deadlineGets a reminder if the list was not submitted.Locks the lists. “To buy” lines from every class go onto one consolidated buy list for the custodian.
SaturdayCollects from the labelled bins, or from a kit tray with their class name. Taps Collected. Bulk items like chart paper are collected as rough quantities.Reduces stock. If “Collected” isn't tapped by Monday, assumes the list was collected as submitted.

B. Something needed mid-fortnight (≈ 1 min)

  • Tap Need something, then pick from the catalogue or type it, and set the date it's needed.
  • If the store has it, the system shows which bin it's in and the educator takes it.
  • If not, it goes on the custodian's buy list, flagged Urgent when needed within 48 hours. Urgent requests are counted, so planning gaps become visible.

C. Had to buy it yourself (rare, ≈ 30 sec)

  • Allowed only for emergencies up to ₹500 (see Rules). Tap I paid, photograph the bill, and check the amount and vendor the system read from it.
  • Tag the class, activity or event. The custodian approves, and the money is returned from the float within 7 days.

D. Event duty (as needed)

  • The event exists in Teacher OS with a budget and a task list. The educator's task shows its materials list against the event budget.
  • Items come from the store first; anything to buy goes through the custodian, or through C if urgent.
  • After the event, tick Returned to store for reusable props and decor. They go back into stock for next time.

One record from end to end (example)

  1. A G1 timetable session on 26 Nov runs an activity whose materials are “chart paper ×6, glue ×1”.
  2. The G1 fortnight list shows 6 chart papers. The store has 4, so 2 go on the buy list.
  3. On Thursday the operations lead (Gurukul) buys a dozen from the wholesaler: one expense of ₹480 with the vendor bill, linked to the buy list.
  4. On Saturday stock goes up by 12. The curriculum lead collects 6, and stock goes down by 6.
  5. The monthly report shows stationery at 62% of budget, with no exceptions on this line.

Every step names the person and the time, and can be traced from the rupee back to the activity, and from the activity forward to the rupee.

Accountability, built in

  • Every record carries who did it and when. Every change goes to the change log, which can't be edited.
  • Each rupee has one owner (the custodian of the float it came from) and one approver.
  • Each store has one owner. Differences found in a count are explained by that owner, or written off with the finance founder's approval.
  • Each event has one owner, who closes it against its budget.
  • Problems are surfaced to the right person automatically. Nobody has to go looking for them.

Risks and how they are handled

  • Staff stop using it, as happened twice before. Logging takes 30 seconds and replaces work they already do. The paper register and the sheet are switched off on fixed dates, so the old way can't drift back.
  • The timetable isn't linked to activities. Until it is, requisitions start from the educator ticking activities. Linking sessions to activities is a Teacher OS priority anyway.
  • Receipt reading is inaccurate. We measure it on real bills before relying on it. The person always confirms the amount.
  • Too many rules at once. Phase 1 has just three: floats, receipts and approval limits. The rest come as each phase goes live.
