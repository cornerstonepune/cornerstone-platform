# Placement

A planning sheet names its rows with labels. A label is not always a subject: it can be a routine, one part of a subject, or
a character area. This standard places one label in one of those four kinds, from the shortlist code gives, so that every
objective and activity under the label lands in the right place.

## The standard

1. **Four kinds only.** A label is a *subject* (taught on its own, with objectives and topics of its own), a *routine* (part
   of the daily rhythm, with no subject or topics), a *part of a subject* (one thing taught inside another subject), or a
   *character area* (how a child feels, wonders, questions or expresses; its objectives are character objectives).
2. **A subject's own name is that subject.** If the label is the name of a subject on the list, choose the subject, not a part
   of it.
3. **A part is not the subject.** If the label names one thing taught inside a subject (rhymes, songs, stories), choose that
   subject's part, never the subject itself and never a different subject.
4. **Routines have no subject.** A label that names a time of the day or a daily practice is a routine, even when its
   activities touch a subject.
5. **Character areas are not subjects.** A label about feeling, curiosity, thinking or expression, with no subject taught, is a
   character area.
6. **Choose one, copied from the list.** The code is copied exactly. At most one is chosen.
7. **Unsure is none.** When the label fits none of the four kinds, or two kinds fit equally, choose none: an empty choice. A
   person places it. A guess is worse than none.
8. **The grade is given, not decided.** The same label can be placed the same way in any grade; the grade changes nothing about
   the kinds.
9. **The reason names the words** of the label and of the choice that decided it, in one sentence. Say "educator", never
   "teacher".
