# Behaviour

A behaviour is something a child does that shows a quality the school cares about, such as being kind, capable or
unafraid. The council reviewed 204 of them as drafts. This standard turns one draft into a character learning objective
(LO): one sentence an educator can see or hear, written the same way as every other LO, plus the near-miss that shows what
"nearly there" looks like. The child is never the problem; the behaviour is not yet there.

## The standard

1. **It begins "The child" and then one verb** of something that can be seen or heard: tells, asks, invites, waits, shares,
   keeps, notices, speaks, listens, goes, raises, changes, and the like. Not "is kind" and not "understands why".
2. **One occasion, one act of the child's own.** What the child does at one time, not what the child usually does.
   Never "always", "never", "often" or "every day".
3. **No other child's name, and no incident.** Say "a classmate". Do not write who did what to whom, or what happened
   last week; describe the kind of moment instead.
4. **Nothing from home, and no disclosure.** No family, no home life, nothing a child told in confidence.
5. **Any language, any way of showing it.** Speech, signing, writing, gesture, a picture or an aid all count. Do not write
   "says" where "shows" is true to the draft, and do not make one language or one mode the only way.
6. **Never these words**: understands, knows, learns, appreciates, is aware of, is exposed to, develops, gets familiar with,
   will help, encourages, borrow (say "exchange" or "regroup"), teacher (say "educator").
7. **A near-miss, not a bad child.** Write what it looks like when the child is nearly there, in the child's act ("Waits
   only when an adult has counted"), never a label ("selfish", "lazy", "rude", "bad"), and never a habit.
8. **Fair for the stage.** The stage is given and is kept: a Foundational child is not asked to lead a team, and a Secondary
   student is not asked to wait for a turn on a slide. If the draft is for another stage, keep the stage given and say what
   a child of that stage does.
9. **Keep the draft's meaning.** Do not add a new quality or a new condition, and do not drop the one the draft has.
10. **Short**: at most 30 words for the behaviour, and one sentence for the near-miss.
